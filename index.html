ddfast design

ddfast is an exact double-dummy solver for bridge. It gives the same answers as DDS, 4 to 5 times faster on one core. These pages explain how, for programmers who know C++ and bridge basics.

The search in brief

ddfast keeps DDS's approach. It asks a sequence of yes/no questions ("can North-South take at least t tricks?") and answers each with an alpha-beta search. Every answer comes with the cards that decided it, so a result is stored as a pattern over just those cards and reused for every position that shares them. DDS's bounds and its ordering of leads are kept too.

What ddfast changes is how this runs: hands are single 64-bit words, the followers' cards are ordered by a few rules, and the last cards of each hand come from tables.

The three ideas

Follow-hand rules · 1.66×

The second, third and fourth hands pick their first card with a few cheap rules instead of DDS's per-card weights. The largest single addition.

One word per hand · 1.52×

Each hand as one 64-bit word, and the card logic as a few bit operations. It pays because of two instructions, pext and pdep.

Endgame tables · 1.30×

Every 2- and 3-card ending is one lookup, and 4-card endings are matched against learned card patterns.
