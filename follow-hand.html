Follow-Hand Rules

The second, third and fourth hands pick their first card with a few cheap rules instead of DDS's per-card weights. It is the largest single thing ddfast adds to DDS.

1.66×slower with DDS's follow weights in place of the rules
−3.4%nodes with DDS's weights: slightly better ordering, at a far higher cost
1 cardis chosen by rule; the rest are tried in a fixed order

The idea

ddfast, like DDS, answers yes/no questions ("can North-South take at least t tricks?") with an alpha-beta search. Where the side to play can refute the question, one good card is enough, and every card tried before it is wasted. So move ordering is mostly about the first card, and in a search of about 140,000 nodes per deal, what that choice costs matters as much as how good it is.

DDS: weigh every card

  1. Compute a weight for each card from the suit's top cards, their owners, the lengths and the trick so far.
  2. Sort the cards by weight.
  3. Try them in that order.

ddfast: one card by rule

  1. Take one candidate per run of touching cards (see One word per hand).
  2. Pick the first card with a short decision list.
  3. Try the rest in a fixed order: lowest first for second and fourth hand, highest first for third hand.

The rules only change the order in which cards are tried, so the answers stay exact. In the examples, South leads, West plays second, North (dummy) third and East fourth; the highlighted card is the rule's pick. Each example was checked by running ddfast's own code on the hands shown.

Why the rules are cheap

DDS's weights need a record of the position (each hand's holdings and lengths, and the top three cards of every suit with their owners), a list of moves, and a weight worked out for every card in one of eleven functions. The rules need none of that:

  • Each test is one compare. "Can partner beat dummy's top card?" compares two hand words masked to the suit led.
  • Each choice is one bit scan. "My lowest card above dummy's top" is a mask and a count of trailing zeros.
  • Only the first card is chosen. The others are simply the lowest or highest bit left in the candidate mask, with no list and no sort.

Second hand

The rules, in the order the code applies them:

  1. Cannot beat the card led: play low.
  2. Partner, playing last, holds three or more cards including one above the lead, dummy's top and my top: play my lowest card above dummy's top, if I have one.
  3. Partner can beat both the lead and dummy's top: play low.
  4. Dummy is void: win with my lowest winner.
  5. Can beat dummy's top: play my lowest card that does.
  6. Otherwise cover with my lowest winner if partner holds nothing above the lead, and play low if partner does.

Third hand

  1. Partner (the leader) is winning: play low, unless fourth hand can beat partner's card. Then play the lowest card above fourth hand's top, or low if there is none.
  2. An opponent is winning: play the lowest card above fourth hand's top. Failing that, the lowest card that beats the current winner. Failing that, low.

Fourth hand

Fourth hand sees the whole trick: it wins with its cheapest winning card, and plays low when partner is already winning or nothing wins.

Void hands

A hand with no card in the suit led ruffs or discards (clubs are trumps in the examples):

  • Second hand ruffs with its lowest winning trump, unless third hand is void too and holds a higher trump. Then it ruffs above third hand's top trump if it can, and otherwise discards.
  • Third hand, with partner winning, ruffs only if fourth hand would beat partner's card.
  • Any hand with an opponent winning ruffs with its lowest winning trump.
  • Otherwise it discards the lowest card of its longest side suit.

What the rules are worth

Replacing the rules with DDS's follow weights (ported line by line), all else equal, on the 600-deal public set and one Zen 5 core (leave-one-out study):

Follow orderingNodesTime
ddfast's rules82.9M15.9M cycles per deal
DDS's follow weights−3.4%1.66× slower

The weights choose slightly better first cards, but every follow node pays to weigh and sort all its cards, and the rules cost a few bit operations. Leads are different: there ddfast keeps DDS's weights, rewritten on the hand words, because a lead's ordering is worth its cost.
