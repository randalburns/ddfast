Follow-Hand Rules

How ddfast chooses the first card for the second, third and fourth hands: a handful of cheap rules in place of DDS's per-card weights. This is the largest single thing ddfast adds to DDS's design.

1.66×slower with DDS's follow weights in place of the rules. LOO
−3.4%fewer nodes with DDS's weights: they order about as well, at a far higher cost. LOO
0.35of ddfast's 4.25× lead over DDS, on a log scale: the largest share of any addition. LOO

Every excerpt is from src/solver.cpp on main, with the default settings. Every example below was checked by running ddfast's own pick_follow and pick_void on the hands shown. Figures come from docs/LOO.md, which measures what each part of ddfast is worth on one Zen 5 core.

Why the first card matters

ddfast, like DDS, asks yes/no questions ("can North-South take at least t tricks?") with an alpha-beta search. At a node where the side to play can refute the question, one good card is enough; every card tried before it is wasted work. Most of the work of move ordering is choosing that first card, and in a search of 140,000 nodes per deal, how much the choice costs matters as much as how good it is.

DDS: weigh every card

  1. For each of the hand's cards, compute a weight from the suit's top cards, who holds them, lengths, and the trick so far (DDS's WeightAlloc functions, one per hand and strain).
  2. Sort the cards by weight.
  3. Try them in that order.

ddfast: one rule-chosen card

  1. Take one candidate per run of touching cards (heads, see One word per hand).
  2. Pick the first card with a short decision list on the hand words.
  3. Try the rest in a fixed order: lowest first for second and fourth hand, highest first for third hand.

Before any card is tried, a second-hand node from 4 cards up asks DDS's second-hand quick tricks, rewritten on the hand words (native_second): whether second hand's side can win this trick and reach its target at once. The rules below order the cards when that does not settle the node.

The rules only change the order in which cards are tried. The answer is exact whatever the order, and the tests check every answer against DDS.

Second hand

In every example South leads, West plays second, North (dummy) third and East fourth. Only the suit that matters is shown; the highlighted card is the one the rule picks. Second hand's rules, in the order the code applies them:

  1. Cannot beat the card led: play low.
  2. Partner (playing last) holds 3+ cards with one above the lead, above dummy's top and above my top: play my lowest card above dummy's top, if I have one.
  3. Partner can beat both the lead and dummy's top: play low.
  4. Dummy (third hand) is void: win with my lowest winner.
  5. Can beat dummy's top: play my lowest card that does.
  6. Partner holds nothing above the lead: cover with my lowest winner. Otherwise play low.

const u64 pl = H[(s + 2) & 3] & lane;                 // partner, playing last
const u64 top3 = nl ? highest(nl) : 0;
const u64 bar = top3 > best ? top3 : best;
if (pl && highest(pl) > bar && popc(pl) >= 3 && highest(pl) > highest(cand)) {
  const u64 w3 = nl ? win & above(highest(nl)) : 0;
  if (w3) return lowest(w3);                         // just above dummy
}
if (pl && highest(pl) > bar) return lowest(cand);  // partner wins: low
if (!nl) return lowest(win);                         // dummy void: win cheaply
u64 w2 = win & above(highest(nl));
if (!w2 && !(H[(s + 2) & 3] & lane & above(best))) return lowest(win);  // cover
return w2 ? lowest(w2) : lowest(cand);

Void hands: ruff or discard

A hand with no card in the suit led either ruffs or discards. In a trump contract (clubs are trumps in these examples; ddfast always keeps trumps in lane 3):

  • Second hand ruffs with its lowest winning trump, unless third hand is void too and holds a higher trump. Then it ruffs above third hand's top trump if it can, and otherwise discards.
  • Third hand, with partner winning, ruffs only if fourth hand would beat partner's card (above fourth hand's top trump if fourth hand is void too).
  • Any hand with an opponent winning ruffs with its lowest winning trump.
  • Otherwise it discards the lowest card of its longest side suit, at no trumps too.

What the rules are worth

Replacing the rules with DDS's follow weights (ported line by line), all else equal, on the 600-deal public set and one Zen 5 core:

Follow orderingNodesCycles per dealSource
ddfast's rules82.9M15.9MLOO
DDS's follow weights−3.4%1.66× slowerLOO

The weights choose slightly better first cards, but every follow node pays to weigh and sort all its cards; the rules cost a few bit operations. Leads are different: there ddfast keeps DDS's weights, rewritten on the hand words, because a lead's ordering pays for its cost.

Reimplementing it

  • Generate one candidate per run of touching cards from the hand words; equivalent cards are never tried twice.
  • Give each follow position its own short decision list for the first card: win cheaply, duck when partner wins, cover when partner cannot, and do not ruff into an overruff.
  • After the first card, try the rest in a fixed order (lowest first for second and fourth hand, highest first for third hand); do not weigh or sort.
  • Keep DDS-quality ordering for leads, where it pays.
